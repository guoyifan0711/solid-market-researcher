# Write Market Analysis Pitch

## Assignment and scope

Read the applicable AGENTS.md, approved page map, source trail, and latest user decisions. Identify the client/SKU, country and category boundary, intended decision, and whether the task is drafting, editing, compression, or review-only. Respect read-only and confirmation/publication boundaries.

For a new assignment or a material change in purpose or scope, use Review's [research intake and question rounds](../review-market-analysis/references/research-usability.md#1-研究启动项与必要追问). Recover existing inputs first. If missing user intent or competing interpretations would change the research route, ask the user before researching or drafting the dependent sections. Research public facts yourself; carry forward clear decisions and authorized discretion.

Read [voice-and-compression.md](references/voice-and-compression.md) before client-copy work. Use [argument-patterns.md](references/argument-patterns.md) when choosing how evidence supports a business judgment; these patterns supply reasoning methods, not category facts or a mandatory page structure.

For taxonomy mistaken for conclusions, source-summary prose, abstract terms or awkward Chinese, use [提案正反例库](references/pitch-before-after.md). Its examples guide sentence choices, not new facts or universal word replacements.

Default to a marketing-proposal market section. Respect an explicit request for a standalone research report, different scope, or different artifact.

## Build the argument at the right level

The whole section should connect:

`Market change → Mechanism → User need/friction → Client opportunity → Marketing task`

Each arrow requires evidence or an explicitly qualified inference. Complete the chain across the section, not on every page or in every supporting bullet.

- A market page may end with a supported market-size, trend, or structural judgment.
- A segment page explains relative growth, drivers, and product-market opportunities without prematurely locking the persona or declaring SKU fit.
- An audience page states priority people, needs, purchase behavior, and media preferences.
- A competition/opportunity page combines market, audience, verified product capabilities, client performance, and peers into marketing priorities.

Before drafting a page, identify its question, evidence boundary, intended judgment, and downstream input. Do not manufacture a client action just to create a bridge, or repeat the client's name where it adds no meaning.

Reuse the audited research boundary. When scope, a key metric or its use changes, follow Review's [研究口径与指标可用性表](../review-market-analysis/references/research-usability.md); when an upstream judgment or shared definition changes, update affected uses through the [跨页依赖表](../review-market-analysis/references/argument-dependencies.md).

## Make the client takeaway explicit

Before writing, state internally what the client should conclude from this page and why it matters at this stage of the proposal. Write a specific answer, such as a market's scale and direction, a segment's relative benefit, a buying barrier or a competitive gap. This working note does not appear in client copy.

- The title states that answer. The Big conclusion explains the central judgment with its decisive evidence. Each Supporting bullet contributes a distinct smaller judgment that helps prove it.
- Source coverage, category definitions, research steps and the fact that a report includes several segments are scope information, not business conclusions. Put them in a short definition or methodological note when needed.
- Cite sources to substantiate the analysis. Do not substitute a sequence of source summaries for the page's reasoning, or append an empty opportunity statement to a fact. A useful inference identifies the specific relationship, mechanism or consequence supported by the evidence.
- Market pages can conclude on scale, direction or structure alone. Do not force a product, audience or marketing implication before that implication has evidence.
- If the evidence cannot sustain the intended judgment, narrow the claim or obtain the missing evidence. Stronger wording cannot repair a missing argument.

Read the title, Big conclusion and Supporting lead sentences together before delivery. The client should be able to say what the page concludes and how the supporting points establish it. If that is unclear, rewrite the page's argument rather than polishing one sentence. Apply this check across the affected Part when feedback identifies a recurring writing problem.

## Evidence into judgment

Define geography, period, unit, category, population, and whether the evidence is Actual, Estimate, Forecast, Scenario, or calculation. Comparisons must preserve these definitions.

Retain information that supports the opportunity's validity or changes a downstream audience, message, product role, proof, content, channel, timing, KPI, or budget decision. Omit interesting facts that do neither.

Use meaningful relationships: category growth versus relative segment growth, long-standing demand versus newly relaxed constraints, purchase needs versus SKU capability, attention versus browsing depth, or actual visitors versus the priority audience. Do not infer causality, market maturity, memory, trust, or sales from a proxy alone.

A verified capability can support a buying reason; differentiation additionally requires a credible competitor comparison. Present a composite persona as an analytical priority, not a joint statistical finding when its attributes come from different samples.

## Compose the requested artifact

- **Short opener:** conclusion-led title and one or two sentences.
- **Single page:** title, Big conclusion, useful visual, supporting bullets, original sources and calculation trail, then longer caveats.
- **Section:** the minimum distinct pages needed for the agreed argument; preserve the user's approved Part boundaries.
- **Review-only:** findings and evidence, not rewritten files unless requested.

### Page order

1. **Conclusion-led title:** answer this page's question in plain business language.
2. **Big conclusion:** usually 1–2 sentences containing the main judgment and essential numbers or comparisons. Use prominent body text or bold, not a quote block.
3. **Visual:** prove the principal relationship; add supporting visuals only when they make another important part of the argument easier to see.
4. **Supporting bullets:** usually 2–3. Each starts with a small conclusion, then identifies the original institution/year and the data or facts supporting it.
5. **Original sources and Calculation Trail:** keep APA originals separate from workbook locations.
6. **Scope/method notes:** collect detailed caveats after the sources; retain material qualifiers beside the relevant claim.

A separate bridge is optional. Transitions should express business relationships, not the next research task. Do not add process language such as instructions to narrow research, verify a later Part, or fix a previous draft.

## Compress without changing meaning

- Merge pages that answer the same question; do not merge distinct market, product, audience, or competitive decisions just to reduce page count.
- Put the important implication into the Big conclusion rather than leaving it hidden in supporting bullets.
- Use only source values or already audited workbook outputs in a drafting-only pass. Do not introduce a new growth rate, share or conversion without its calculation trail; if the request only asks for copy, use the supplied original values instead.
- With only two endpoint observations, describe the end-to-end change. Words such as “持续／逐年／连续增长” require intervening observations or an original source that explicitly supports that continuity.
- Keep short qualifiers such as country, sample, estimate, or forecast when removing them would strengthen the claim improperly.
- Respect a page's scope through what it says. Do not append a disclaimer about absent SKU, audience or media evidence to a macro conclusion that does not make those claims; such later-stage gaps belong in internal notes.
- Move raw derivations, audit status, long definitions, research gaps, exhaustive comparisons, and method detail into notes or workpapers.
- Prefer active, readable commercial Chinese. Use one judgment or inferential step per sentence and define professional terms.
- Do not rewrite an adverse finding into an optimistic opportunity without evidence. Do not leave rebuttals to discarded drafts in client copy.
- An Executive Summary reflects the body: current market scale, future space, priority opportunity/audience, client gap and marketing task as relevant. It cannot introduce a new number or judgment.

## Copy-level self-check

Check the argument first, then read the complete revised sentences and paragraphs aloud. With reviewer suggestions, choose what to adopt or adjust and re-read the resulting prose; word-level replacement alone does not finish the edit.

Before delivery:

- Does the page answer its assigned question without exceeding its layer?
- Can the client state the page's judgment and why it matters, without having to infer the conclusion from a list of data or source summaries?
- Is the main finding understandable quickly, including the important number or comparison?
- Do several supporting conclusions actually build the Big conclusion?
- Are facts, calculations, inference, recommendations and forecast/scenario distinguishable?
- Are the original sources identifiable and the calculation trail separate?
- Are internal process remarks absent and material caveats retained?
- Does the complete section provide the inputs for the subsequent marketing proposal?

This check covers copy, not a full source, workbook, visual, or publication audit. Use the applicable project review workflow for those tasks; do not claim comprehensive verification from a writing pass.
