# Voice and Compression Standard

## 1. Commercial voice and page boundary

Write as a market-facing partner: plain, evidence-led Chinese that can be presented aloud. The whole argument runs from market change through needs and opportunity to marketing tasks. Individual pages only answer their assigned question.

The market layer may state what is happening and why it matters for the opportunity. It does not need a sentence beginning with the client's name. Audience and competitive pages supply the more specific WHO and HOW when the evidence is ready.

## 2. Titles and Big conclusions

A title states the answer, not a subject heading or research method. Depending on the page, it can identify market direction, relative segment growth, a priority audience, a competitor position or a marketing task.

Use 1–2 sentences in the Big conclusion:

- State the supported judgment directly, including the key magnitude or comparison when central.
- Add the main implication or condition only when it belongs to this layer and is supported.

Do not force the same “opportunity + condition for winning” formula on every page. Do not turn the conclusion into a list of research constraints. Use a clear heading and prominent text; quote blocks are for actual quotations, not Big conclusions.

For a market-only page with verified comparable original values, a complete conclusion can simply say: “行业估计显示，全球市场规模从起点数值扩大到终点数值，市场基础持续扩张。” Insert the actual source values and years. Do not add a claim about unstudied buyers or a disclaimer listing every later-stage question; retain only qualifications material to the market statement itself.

In copy-only work, use approved values. New percentages, CAGR or ratios need an audited calculation trail before entering client copy; do not casually calculate a new headline number while polishing prose.

## 3. Supporting bullets

Default construction:

**小结论。原始机构（年份）统计／指出／表明具体 data & facts。**

Several independent supporting conclusions together support the page-level judgment. A bullet may combine related sources when they contribute to one mechanism; it should not read as a research diary.

Test each lead sentence as a business judgment: does it say something specific about scale, change, relative performance, need, constraint or choice? The evidence that follows must prove that sentence. A category list, a description of a report's coverage or an explanation of the analyst's next step does not pass this test.

Use the analyst's reasoning directly. State the supported relationship, then give the source and facts. Cut a closing “这说明／这表明” sentence if it merely repeats the fact or adds an unspecified “机会／潜力／需求基础”. Keep an inference when it explains a specific consequence and the evidence supports the step. Do not turn editorial confidence into stronger causal or strategic claims.

- Put the interpretation first, then show the evidence that earns it.
- Keep exact numbers, denominators, populations and dates needed for credibility.
- Identify the original institution, not just “Excel calculation” or “research shows.”
- When using calculated results, cite the original inputs in the bullet or source block and locate the formula in Calculation Trail.
- State cross-validation precisely: it may confirm business value, direction, or mechanism, without independently proving the same market size.
- Show important facts in the visual when they change the headline's time coverage or comparison; do not bury an essential newer period in one bullet.

## 4. Compress the reasoning

For internal editing, tag sentences:

- F: fact or calculated observation.
- M: mechanism or structural interpretation.
- U: user need, friction or behavior.
- O: client opportunity or relative choice.
- A: marketing task.
- B: boundary or uncertainty.
- P: research process or revision history.

Keep the elements appropriate to this page: market pages often need F + M; audience pages add U; competition and synthesis pages connect to O + A. The full section completes the chain.

Retain only facts that support the page judgment; combine related facts into a mechanism where defensible. Remove P from client copy. Move long B to source-end notes, while keeping essential qualifiers beside the claim.

An instruction to verify something in a later Part belongs in a task card or workpaper. A natural transition can describe the business relationship without exposing the work plan.

## 5. Prevent inference inflation

- A growth slowdown does not independently prove maturity or a share war.
- Market-wide tailwinds do not independently prove relative growth of one product.
- A policy changes an adoption condition only for the relevant jurisdiction, product and eligible user.
- Audience demographics from separate samples do not prove one combined population.
- Reach, media preference, self-reported influence and measured conversion are different.
- Competitor visibility does not independently prove category education is complete.
- Low return visits do not independently prove weak trust or brand memory.
- A product feature can support relevance; uniqueness needs relative evidence.
- A mechanism hypothesis can guide a test; it cannot be written as measured causal impact.

## 6. Source and caveat placement

Place sources near the relevant page or Part:

- **Main Source:** APA original author/institution, available date, title, URL; page/table locator as useful.
- **Cross-validation:** APA original source plus what it validates.
- **Product/regulatory evidence:** original specification, certificate, law or regulator, with applicable SKU/jurisdiction.
- **Calculation basis:** the original sources used for derived results.
- **Calculation Trail:** workbook, Sheet/Range, formula, denominator and assumptions, linked back to original Source IDs.

A workbook is not an independent source institution. Do not invent publication dates or claim to have acquired a paid report when only its public summary was read.

Long methodology and limitations go after sources. Country, sample, Estimate/Forecast/Scenario and other qualifiers that materially change the claim remain next to it. Professional terms should receive a useful definition in the appendix, not only an English expansion.

## 7. Read-aloud and final edit

- Can the reader repeat the page's point after ten seconds?
- Does the Big conclusion include the important finding, rather than only a vague summary?
- Does each supporting bullet begin with a distinct contribution to that finding?
- Reading only the title, Big conclusion and bullet leads, can the client repeat a clear judgment and its main reasons? If not, revise the argument across the affected page or Part.
- Could the wording be mistaken for a causal claim, national fact, actual sales or measured ROI when the evidence is narrower?
- Have the user's accepted changes carried through the title, text, visual and Summary?
- Does this page contribute to the whole proposal without repeating another page?

Use examples as writing moves, not reusable sector claims. Preserve the user's requested artifact size and confirmation boundary.
