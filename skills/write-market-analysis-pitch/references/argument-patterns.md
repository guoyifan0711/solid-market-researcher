# Reusable argument patterns

These are generic reasoning patterns. Choose the ones relevant to the decision and support each application with current evidence.

## Define the market before sizing it

Fix the geography, customer, application and revenue or volume boundary before using a market estimate. Adjacent categories can explain context without entering the addressable-market total. Definitions belong near the numbers and in the source trail.

## Explain what changed

A long-standing need does not explain recent growth by itself. Identify the constraint that changed, the mechanism through which it affects adoption, and the conditions likely to persist. Explain relative segment growth using comparable observations or a segment-specific mechanism. A shared macro trend alone does not establish a winner.

## Separate demand, accessibility and capture

Establish the need or demand pool, then assess whether the audience can buy and use the offering, and what would make the client a credible choice. A large pool, good product fit and high brand share answer different questions. Keep them distinct when setting priorities.

## Compare regions through the buyer's decision

Compare the underlying need, local market stage, buying trigger, product requirements, channel access and adoption barriers. Group regions only where those decisive conditions are similar. Explain the important exceptions before reusing an audience or tactic across them.

## Translate policy into a concrete purchase condition

Identify who is eligible, what cost or step changes, when the change applies and which product is covered. Separate an announced measure from an effective rule, and product eligibility from verified compliance.

## Use observed gaps to define the marketing task

Comparable measures can reveal where attention, adoption or repeat use falls short. Describe the observed gap first. Test explanations against alternative causes before assigning a tactic or psychological interpretation. Match channel recommendations to the evidence available for reach, preference, reported influence or measured conversion.

## Pick benchmarks for the decision

A competitor can be useful as a scale benchmark, a same-tier alternative or an execution reference. State the role and compare the relevant dimensions. Product relevance supports a buying reason; a claim of uniqueness also needs credible competitor evidence.

## Make the audience actionable

Name the priority people, task, buying trigger, barriers, decision path and media behavior with source and sample scope. Attributes from separate studies can inform a test audience, but they do not establish a measured joint persona.

## Compress by the number of decisions

Merge pages when their evidence answers the same buyer or business question. Keep separate pages when they support different decisions. The complete section supplies the marketing implications; each individual page stays within its own evidence boundary.
